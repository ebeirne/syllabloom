A clear-eyed comparison · Study workflow

If an AI can make cards, what is Syllabloom for?

A capable chat model can draft flashcards from notes. Syllabloom is not a claim that our model is smarter. The bet is a less scattered path from one class’s files and dates to cards you can inspect, study, and take into Anki.

Cards alone are easy to copy

If you already use Claude, ChatGPT, or another assistant, you can ask it to draft questions from a slide deck. That can be a perfectly reasonable way to get started. Syllabloom cannot win just by putting a flashcard prompt behind a new button.

The harder student problem often comes after the first draft: Which file did this fact come from? Is it on the syllabus? Is there an exam soon? Which cards have I already studied? Can I edit the wording and export only the set I want? Those little jobs spread across folders, chats, spreadsheets, and Anki unless something keeps the course together.

Compare the job, not an AI leaderboard

This is a workflow comparison, not a benchmark of answer quality. Use whichever tool gives you accurate cards you are comfortable studying.

A chat assistant
Useful for a focused request or rewriting a difficult idea. You decide how to keep the source, syllabus dates, edits, study history, and export settings organized around a course.
Anki
A mature spaced-repetition app with a broad card and scheduling system. Syllabloom prepares an editable, class-oriented set that can be exported to Anki; it does not replace Anki’s review scheduler.
Syllabloom
A one-class workspace for syllabus dates, course materials, source-linked cards, in-app study, and an Anki export you can inspect and edit first.

The wedge: keep the source close to the question

A card with no context can sound plausible and still be wrong for the lecture. A source label gives you a route back to the slide, document, or note that produced it. Then you can check whether the prompt tests the right idea, whether the answer adds anything unsupported, and whether it is worth reviewing.

Source traceability is not an accuracy certificate. Card quality still needs to be evaluated on real course files, and the student should review, correct, or remove cards before relying on them. The product should earn trust by saving the checking work—not by asking you to trust a confident-looking answer.

What would make Syllabloom worth keeping?

  1. It can read a real class’s source files and keep useful context attached to the resulting notes and cards.
  2. It can bring syllabus dates into a calendar and use upcoming assessments to shape a manageable study session.
  3. It lets you inspect the entire set, edit or delete weak cards, study in the app, or export to the tool you already use.
  4. It is honest when dates are unclear, when a source has no dated schedule, and when recording transcription is not available.

Those are the parts beta users should test. Does this remove enough organizing and deck-prep friction from one difficult class to justify adding another step to your routine? If not, the product has more work to do.

Honest beta limits

The current beta is free for one active class and has no paid checkout. Syllabus dates and card drafts still need your review. Audio and video remain on the device; hosted transcription and automatic recording-based cards are not enabled. Account sign-in depends on the deployment’s Clerk configuration, so confirm the sign-in notice on the build you are using.

Anki export is there to preserve your choice, not lock you in. If an assistant you already use makes a better card for a particular topic, keep it. The question for Syllabloom is whether it can make the whole class loop more useful, not whether every single prompt is better than a human-edited one.

What we should measure

  • Time from upload to a usable reviewed deck.
  • Share of cards students keep after editing.
  • Whether source links help find and fix errors.
  • Whether students return before the next exam.

What we should not claim yet

Better grades, perfect accuracy, superior AI, or a card set that needs no review. Those require evidence from real beta use.
