Why copying a whole slide makes a weak card

A slide may mix a definition, an example, a diagram, and a heading. Pasting all of that onto one card tests recognition of a paragraph instead of recall of a useful idea. A stronger card gives one prompt enough context to stand on its own and one answer that explains the point without adding facts the course source never gave.

For a comparison, ask about the distinction. For a process, ask for the steps or the key cause-and-effect link. If a term is easy to confuse, include the unit or topic in the question. The goal is not to make the largest deck; it is to make the smallest set of questions that lets you retrieve the course’s important ideas.

The excerpt below is an illustrative economics example, not material from a student’s class upload. Notice how the question stays close to the sentence instead of asking for a broad definition of an entire topic.

In the app, source labels help you return to the uploaded document or slide. A source pointer helps with checking; it is not a guarantee that every generated question is accurate or useful. Read each card before you rely on it.

A practical slide-to-Anki workflow

  1. Add the class context. Start with one course, then add the syllabus and relevant slides, notes, readings, or past assessments.
  2. Check what the source produced. Review the prepared cards and their source labels. Keep a prompt only if the question is clear and its answer is supported by the material.
  3. Edit or remove cards. Make wording more specific, correct a detail from the source, or delete a card that is redundant or not useful.
  4. Export the set you chose. Configure the Anki format, deck name, tags, and limits. The beta creates an Anki package after your review.

Starting from course files can save the blank-page work, while the review step keeps the student responsible for what they actually study. If the slide deck has no useful explanation for a topic, add the lecture notes or reading that fills the gap instead of asking a card generator to guess.

Questions students ask

Can I use the cards without Anki?

Yes. The beta includes an in-app study flow, and Anki export is optional. Your card set remains reviewable and editable before you choose an export.

Can I choose Basic or cloze cards?

The Anki setup includes card format preferences, deck and tag settings, and export limits. Check the resulting set before importing it into Anki.

Does a recording become cards automatically?

Not in the public beta. Audio and video recordings stay on the current device; hosted transcription and recording-based card generation are not enabled yet.

The useful difference

Card generation is only the first step. Keep source checking, editing, in-app study, and Anki export together around one class.

Keep the review honest

  • Check that each prompt stands alone.
  • Confirm the answer against the source.
  • Remove duplicates and low-value facts.
