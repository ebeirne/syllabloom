# Syllabloom student demo runbook

Use this with 5 to 8 medical students. Give no product tour before the tasks. The goal is to learn whether the workflow explains itself and whether the resulting cards earn trust.

## Setup

- Open the saved Human Anatomy class.
- Keep the supplied muscle document, `test-output/rounds-medical-slide-import-test.pptx`, and the saved Tibialis anterior lecture available.
- Start each student with the Today screen.
- Ask permission before recording notes or the session.

## Tonight's 20-minute acceptance

1. Open Class → Materials and upload `rounds-medical-slide-import-test.pptx`.
2. Confirm the source row says `5 slides`, `230 words`, `2 objective cues`, and `9 card drafts`.
3. Open Review without coaching. Ask the student what `COURSE SOURCE · SLIDE 2` means.
4. Have the student edit one PowerPoint-derived card, accept it, and leave one unwanted card out.
5. Export accepted cards to a temporary Anki deck and verify the source field names the PowerPoint and slide. Do not import into the student's real deck unless they want to.
6. Start Study, reveal the answer, and rate it.
7. Open Record and complete a 30-second microphone test: start, mark, pause, resume, and finish.
8. Ask the student to compare the generated wording with how they would personally write the same cards.

Pass tonight only if the student can complete steps 1–7 with no more than one rescue, the slide citations make sense, and they would keep at least six of the nine drafts after editing.

## Student tasks

1. Import the PowerPoint and find its draft cards.
2. Explain the slide citation and identify which source produced the answer.
3. Edit one card until it matches how you would write it for yourself.
4. Accept that card and leave one unwanted card out.
5. Export the accepted card to Anki and inspect its source field.
6. Start a study round, reveal an answer, and rate it.
7. Open Record and begin a 30-second test lecture recording.
8. Mark one important moment, pause, resume, and finish the recording.
9. Tell us what happened to the audio and what the source status means.
10. Return to Today and explain why the recommended topic was selected.

Do not rescue the student unless they are stuck for 30 seconds. Record where they looked first and what wording caused uncertainty.

## What to record

| Measure | Pass signal |
| --- | --- |
| Starts classroom recording | Under 10 seconds and without coaching |
| Understands recording state | Can pause, resume, mark, and finish without coaching |
| Understands `COURSE SOURCE` vs `REVIEW ONLY` | Explains the difference without coaching |
| Finds lecture cards | Under 20 seconds |
| Edits and accepts a card | Completes without help |
| Exports to Anki | Completes without help |
| Understands the study recommendation | Can name the evidence behind it |
| Card usefulness | Would personally keep at least 2 of 3 drafts after editing |
| Trust | Rates confidence at least 4 out of 5 after checking the source |

## Questions after the tasks

1. What felt immediately useful?
2. What made you hesitate or distrust a card?
3. What would you need before using this during a real lecture?
4. Which step felt slower than your current workflow?
5. Would you use this weekly? If not, what is the deal-breaker?

## Decision rule

Do not add more features after one interview. After at least five sessions, fix the three failures that recur most often. The current concept passes the first student gate only if at least four of five students can edit and export a source-backed card without help, and at least three say they would use the workflow weekly.
